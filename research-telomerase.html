Research / Telomerase regulation

Project

Telomerase regulation by DNA helicases

Telomeres — the repetitive DNA-protein caps at chromosome ends — must be maintained long enough to protect the genome, but not so long that they promote unchecked proliferation. Telomerase, the enzyme that extends telomeric DNA, is itself regulated by DNA helicases and telomere-binding proteins. We study how Hrq1 and Pif1 modulate telomerase activity in vitro, and how the telomere-binding protein Cdc13 coordinates dynamic DNA exchange at telomeric DNA.

What we've found

We showed that Hrq1 and Pif1 synergistically modulate telomerase activity in vitro, and more recently turned to Cdc13, the yeast telomere end-binding protein, showing that it exhibits dynamic DNA strand exchange on telomeric DNA and that Cdc13 dimerization is essential for that exchange — a mechanistic link between how Cdc13 recognizes telomeric DNA and how telomerase is regulated at the chromosome end.
