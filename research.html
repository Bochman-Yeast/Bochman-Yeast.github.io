Research

Genome stability, one helicase at a time

Every genome must be faithfully copied, repaired, and protected across generations. DNA helicases — motor proteins that use ATP hydrolysis to unwind double-stranded DNA — sit at the center of that work. Despite decades of study, the in vivo roles of many helicases remain unresolved, even though mutations in several human helicases cause disease. We study helicases from bacteria to humans: how they work biochemically, what they do inside cells, and why losing them leads to genome instability, disease, and aging.

RecQ4 helicases & genome stability

Biochemistry and cell biology of the yeast RecQ4-family helicase Hrq1 and its human ortholog RECQL4, whose dysfunction is linked to Rothmund-Thomson syndrome and cancer predisposition.

Telomerase regulation by DNA helicases

How helicases and telomere-binding proteins (Pif1, Hrq1, Cdc13) coordinate to control telomerase access and telomere length.

Post-translational modification of Pif1

How acetylation and other modifications regulate Pif1 helicase activity, including at G-quadruplex DNA structures.

Fermentation science

Wild yeast bioprospecting and brewing/fermentation microbiology, including the community-science Declaration of Fermentation project.
