Research / Pif1 post-translational modification

Project

How post-translational modifications affect Pif1 helicase activity

Pif1 is a genome caretaker found across the tree of life, from bacteria to humans, and it suppresses genome instability at G-quadruplex DNA structures. Its activity isn't fixed — it can be tuned after translation. We study how modifications such as lysine acetylation regulate Pif1's biochemical activity, and how the helicase's own domain architecture and the DNA sequence and structure it encounters shape what it does.

What we've found

We showed that lysine acetylation regulates the activity of nuclear Pif1 and that dynamic regulation of Pif1 acetylation is crucial for maintaining genome stability. We've also mapped how Pif1's N-terminal domain regulates its biochemical activities, and how DNA sequence and structure — including G-quadruplexes — modulate Pif1 activity, building on the original discovery that Pif1-family helicases suppress genome instability at G-quadruplex motifs.
