Research / RecQ4 helicases

Project

RecQ4 helicases & genome stability

RecQ-family helicases are conserved genome caretakers, and mutations in the human RecQ4 helicase RECQL4 cause Rothmund-Thomson syndrome and predispose carriers to cancer. We use the budding yeast RecQ4-family helicase Hrq1 as a tractable model to dissect how this helicase family contributes to DNA repair and genome stability, and how its dysfunction can turn a genome caretaker into a liability.

What we've found

Hrq1 shares structural and functional homology with human RECQL4 and stimulates the Pso2 translesion nuclease to promote DNA interstrand crosslink repair. We've mapped Hrq1's genetic and physical interaction networks, screened for genetic interactions with hrq1Δ and hrq1ΔN alleles, and — most recently — shown that particular DNA-binding states can convert an otherwise inactive RecQ4-family helicase into a dominant-negative DNA repair factor, a mechanism with direct relevance to how disease-linked RECQL4 mutations might behave in human cells.
